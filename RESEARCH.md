## 2. The circuit

Marvel Rivals Ignite 2026 is the official pro circuit (NetEase and Marvel Games) and the first year of the Marvel Rivals Professional League, with a circuit prize pool above $3,000,000. Four premier regions: Americas, EMEA, China, and Pacific (Asia and Oceania merged for 2026, playing separate group stages and merged playoffs). League size per stage: Americas 12, EMEA 12, China 8, Pacific-Asia 8, Pacific-Oceania 8, for 48 spots. Twelve partner organizations (Americas 7, EMEA 3, Pacific 2, China 0) hold guaranteed spots: 100 Thieves, FlyQuest, Gen.G Esports, Liquid Citadel, Natus Vincere, NRG Shock, REJECT, Sentinels, Spacestation Gaming, Team Heretics, TSM, Virtus.pro. The path to pro is the Marvel Rivals Championship (MRC), an in-game open circuit running roughly every two months, feeding the league through a promotion and relegation system from Season 7 onward. League Points from Stage 1, the Mid-Season Finals and Stage 2 (weighted heaviest) decide Grand Finals qualification alongside the Stage 2 playoff winners. (Liquipedia's Ignite 2026 hub; the official announcement on marvelrivalsesports.com; Esports Insider's roadmap coverage; Dot Esports' participating list.)

## 4. Match stats: what exists and where (corrected by the completed research pass)

Two different kinds of data exist, and they are not equally available:

- **Series and map results ARE structured.** Liquipedia (and its LPDB) publishes series scores, per-map scores, map names and modes, dates, placements and League Points. This is the reliable day-one feed for fixtures, results, the Squad slot, and advancement facts.
- **Per-player box scores are NOT published as data anywhere found.** The full stat line the game produces per map (eliminations, deaths, assists, final hits, damage dealt, damage blocked, healing, hero played, MVP and SVP) appears only on the in-game scoreboard shown during official broadcasts and VODs. NetEase publishes no stats page, API or downloadable box score for pro matches, and Liquipedia does not carry per-player lines. Community trackers (Tracker Network, RivalsMeta and similar) aggregate ranked ladder stats, which are NOT pro-match stats and are never substituted for them here.

Consequence for the product, stated plainly: player scoring needs box scores, and box scores today come from extraction (manual or vision-assisted) off the official broadcast record. The import pipeline is built for exactly that: results enter through `api/admin/import-results.js`, every payload must name its source (a broadcast VOD and timestamp is a valid source), and unresolved references are reported back, never guessed or dropped. Wave 2 work: the results pipeline that pulls structured series and map results from Liquipedia and attaches extracted per-map player lines for Stage 2, each line carrying its source. Until real imports land, the schedule page says fixtures publish with the competition, and no page presents demo or illustrative numbers as real stats.

Note: direct page fetches to liquipedia.net returned 403 from this build environment, so wave-1 verification leaned on Esports Insider, Dot Esports, Beebom, DreamHack, Esports.gg and team sites read directly, with the two research passes cross-checked against each other; every conflict resolved above went to the source with the stronger provenance.

## 5. The League of Legends fantasy model

Fantasy LCS (Riot's official game, shut down ahead of Spring 2019) is the template, per the user's order. Its published scoring table (2016 and 2017 guides, identical): kill 2, death -0.5, assist 1.5, creep score 0.01 per CS; bonuses for a triple kill (+2), quadra kill (+5) and penta kill (+10), and +2 for a 10+ kill-or-assist game. A "Team" item scored objectives instead of a player: tower 1 each, dragon 1 each, baron 2 each, first blood 2, win 2, and +2 for a win in under 30 minutes (2017 table). Roster shape: five role starters, one Flex, one Team item, three alternates, with at most 2 players at any one position across the roster. League sizes were 4, 6 or 8. Draft: snake (order reverses each round). Format: weekly head-to-head matchups, and the best head-to-head record won the league; no total-points mode appears in the sources (a named gap in the underlying research). Lineups locked per region at that region's first match of the week, so EU and NA players locked on different days. Counting in the best-of era was a pre-draft league choice between "First Two Games" and "Best Game". There were no native playoffs: Riot's FAQ is explicit that only regular-season matches counted. Riot shut the game down after running it for two years without committing resources to improve it; a search for a partner to rebuild it failed, and it never returned. (Sources: Polygon's 2016 and 2017 Fantasy LCS guides, Riot's Fantasy LCS FAQ and support pages, PCGamesN, Engadget, Twinfinite, Dot Esports on the shutdown.)

**Translation decisions (also in ARCHITECTURE.md):** kill, death and assist values carry over untouched where they translate (eliminations 2.0, deaths -0.5, assists 1.5). The hero-shooter stat lines the game produces per map (section 4) take the place of CS: final hits 0.5, damage, blocked and healing on per-thousand rates. The Team item becomes the Squad slot: draft one pro org in a final round; it scores map wins, series wins and clean sweeps. Counting deviates from Best Game on purpose: a Marvel Rivals week is one series per team, so every series counts. Playoffs deviate too: the original had none, and a fantasy league needs an ending, so the top four play semifinals and a final.

Successors and benchmarks: DraftKings runs salary-cap daily fantasy for League of Legends. Esports One (2020) kept the five positions plus Team slot but dropped exclusivity for a salary cap, and added a captain and champion-select bonuses. Sleeper has run a Fantasy LCS since 2021: season-long head-to-head with an exclusive snake draft, five starters (one per role, no Flex, Team slot or bench in the sources found), champion pick/ban multipliers, and default scoring of kill 3, death -1, assist 2. Correction, recorded plainly: an early note in this project's research called Sleeper's League of Legends fantasy unconfirmed. The completed research pass verified it from Dot Esports' Fantasy LCS guides and Nerd Street's draft coverage, and this document follows the verified version. Summoner's Inn Fantasy (Freaks 4U, 2023 onward) is another living season-long successor.

## 6. Deadlock

Verified: Deadlock has **no official league and no Valve-run circuit** as of this build, and the game itself is still unreleased and invite-only. What exists is a real community scene: Deadlock Night Shift, a weekly invitational in separate EU and NA brackets (at edition 59 by late September 2026), with a base prize that ran about $300 historically and $2,000 to $2,500 per week in mid-2026 editions, plus a $12,000 Night Shift Open in May 2026 and occasional $10,000 opens. Most recurring top teams are unaffiliated stacks, not org rosters; Virtus.pro signed a roster in February 2025 and dropped it that August, citing limited clarity about the game's competitive future. Peak concurrent players hit 262,145 on Oct 9, 2026 (SteamDB via Inven Global), so the audience is real even though the league is not. Sources: Liquipedia (Deadlock portal and Night Shift pages), Esports.gg, Deadfrag, PSU, Showgamer, Inven Global. The data model is game-agnostic from day one (every entity is game-scoped), and Deadlock is registered in the seed as a watched game with no competitions, teams or players. Its section opens when a real pro scene exists, per the user's framing.
